### How much time should I work?

 In a light of recent progress with time spent working/sacrificing present to the future - I need to have a proper thought about this because I see how this race for time can get pathological very quickly.

\[SPOILER]

**What do I mean by work?**

 Sacrificing present to the future. Kinda is a biblical definition.

**Differentiation of the work**

 There's hobby-work. There's project work. There's studying work. And so on.

**Time budget**

 I know for sure that 8 hours is too much for me. 6 hours is probably pushing the limit. So I'll focus on 7 hours. Which means from the time I open my eyes to the time I fall asleep I have 17 hours.

**Necessities**

 - I need 30 minutes per day to maintain the Order in the house.

 - I need 2 hours per day for cooking & eating.

 - I need 30 minutes per day to play with my cat. We both stay active, we both have fun, it's surprisingly is a pretty important thing.

 - I need 30 minutes in the morning on average for routine. Write dreams (if any), wash my face, make a coffee with butter, clean cat's litter box.

 - I need 30 minutes before going to sleep for routine. Setup bed, take a shower, fall asleep.

**Real time budget**

 13 hours.

---

**Why I need the time so much**

 I heard Peterson say that people who can achieve success in their domain spend 80 hours per week working flat out. And that became my number for positioning myself. 80 hours per week is 11.5 hours per day every day. But why not doing insane hours makes me like uncomfortable or anxious. Because weak progress kills. Like I'm already in a vulnerable position. If I won't work as much as possible and as efficient as possible - my chances to be wiped out by a black swan event is increasing drastically, which I don't appreciate.

**Is working 12-13 hours per day is really what you want?**

 Yesterday when I have very good working day, when I was on 8-hour mark, laying on the floor to reset a brain. I found myself thinking about watching youtube or PMO or play games. Those didn't come nowhere near to what I had that day. Like the most dopaminergic activities were inferior to have a meaningful day of productive work. Like what else can I do? Play games to see their faults and start thinking about writing my own game? Watch youtube just for the sake of watching with nothing useful extracted from there? (besides examples of human behavior, since those are rare in my isolation). Even PMO was weaker than that because I knew that it's effects are fleeting and can harm me in further work.

 So it's not like I have people to meet, or family to attend to, or go out with a girlfriend/wife, or play with kids, or have fun with friends. Like I literally don't have anything else meaningful. So do I want working for 12-13 hours per day? No at the surface. But deeply - YES, because it gives insane amount of meaning compared to other activities I can do. And meaning sustains the life, helps to persevere through rough patches, helps to deal with pain (physical or physiological).

**Why hours though?**

 The thing is - without hours it's easier to fall into suboptimal paths by saying like "oh today I worked effectively 16 hours, (but 10 hours on paper), so I can watch youtube some more". While by focusing on 12 hours+ - it keeps me in a good kind of stress even towards the night.

**But what you feel now? It's not positive feeling**

 Yeah, it's as if I'm missing something. I guess it's about Jung. Like I need at least 1-2 hours per day spent on either dream analysis OR studying psychoanalysis. Like this shit works well, but if I won't get any new knowledge - I'll start to invent my own "interpretation" I will drift and the practice won't be as effective (if at all).

 So what I feel is that I need to progress beyond work, to make it sustainable. Like I need to become better human being WHILE having maximum progress on the work. Like I can spare 2 hours to still stay within magic 80 hours per week. From those - 30 minutes on interoceptive meditation. And 60-90 minutes on efficient studying. Am I happy with it now?

**Yeah**

 I don't feel that resistance now. It seems to be what I need. If I'll be getting better and psychoanalysis, not only I'll be able to increase chances of having this work in a consistent manner, but also efficiency will increase as well, because complexes will be less interfering with suboptimal paths.

**Exercising though**

 Yeah... 2 days ago I tried again on 6.5 hours of sleep, and it wasted me. But at the same time it felt great. Like I have this pain in muscles and it feels pretty nice, because it's like indication that body gets stronger as well. And I don't need yoga-stuff, I need like properly high HR thing. But at the same time It takes 45 minutes at best.

**But what Peterson means by working 80 hours per week flat out?**

 What if I'm already working much more than those people? like I'm not distracted by other people. Psychoanalysis allows me to progressively decrease distraction by negative complexes. Dream analysis specifically, allows me to avoid productivity pitfalls. Lots of meaning - gives me lots of speed and efficiency. And honesty allows to see things for what they are, so I can constantly optimize productivity even further. Like that's a thing. What if this way of living gives me upper hand even on "successful" people and like my 80 hours could be theirs 120?

 I really think that I must adopt that attitude. To not work 80 hours per week, but to work 80 hours per week, and beat successful people's 80 hours through sheer efficiency. And also chances are there's breaks counted in those 80 hours. While in my case it's distilled 80 hours of pure work. Because that's how mentally healthy people with stable lifes count work. 9-5 is 8 hours of work. But if you really see how much work is there on average + a lunch + additional breaks + meetings + etc. - there's like really 2-3 hours of work.

 Am I unreasonable? I'll ask AI and will explicitly tell it to be critical. - Yeah. I am unreasonable. Because through meetings a company can delegate 100 hours of work. While I have to do it alone. And I'm not competing with a someone - I'm competing with a companies. So I need to stay humble in here. I may achieve high efficiency, but successful people have other people working for them and I don't. Which means even though I may reach higher efficiency, but I can't a company of 100 people even if they work 10 hours per day and have all that big company inertia. 

 But that's not the point, isn't it. Like what I'm saying is that if you put "successful" kind of productivity guy in MY place - chances are that I will beat him in that productivity. But like who is that guy? Like he can be much more experienced software dev. And then he can work 1/2 of my time and implement more. That's why it's a BS math. I can't compare anyone to myself, because there's like a million different variables that come with that comparison.

**Full Reset**

 Ok.

 First of all - **Orientation on the Present**. There's a lot of future/theoretical talk. Focus on the present. Here and now. Work this hours efficiently. And then next one WHEN you'll get to that next one.

 Then - **Drop 80 hours per week**. It's theoretical future-promise. Give your fullest TODAY. And then on the next day WHEN it arrives. You can't compare your 80 hours per week with other peoples. Like even if you can imagine that your efficiency is at 99.999th percentile in the world - A "successful" with team of 10 professional soft.devs being at 50th percentile of productivity might overtake you (because like they can be burned out, vibe code, unmotivated, without meaning + company inertia on top of that). But still - absolute efficiency doesn't mean nothing comparison-wise.

 3rd - **4 hours necessities + 2 hours of studying & meditation + 45 minutes exercising**. If you stop counting - you have a good life to live that covers all your needs. Yeah, you'll have ~10 hours of pure work and 70 hours per week. But it's just numbers that don't mean anything. If you didn't "waste" last 2-3 months on inner work, and indulgence loops struggle - you wouldn't work now at all. And before that - you was doing "90+ hours per week", while real efficiency was like 1/5 of what you have now. So hours mean shit.

**Side note on meditation**

 Learned about the Jung's Four Functions. Seems like my inferior function is the Sensation. So yesterday I got a thought, sensation is tied to Central Executive Network(focusing attention on sensations) in the brain which is antagonistic with Default Mode Network(creative thinking/brainstorming). My work is highly dependent on latter. So why not to meditate by activating former. Previous meditations was like allowing DMS to just flow and flood my mind with images and thoughts. But yesterday I tried focusing on sensations, and looks like it allowed DMS to calm down and "recover" because after that meditation I got surge of meaning to continue the work. I have such crash out around 8th-9th hour into the day - so if I need reset - now meditation has truly proven way to be really useful specifically for my case (and not generally). 

 Though this idea is very shallow. And of course there's more stuff involved besides just neurophysiology. But this point just makes a lot of sense to me. Like it has huge benefit through neurophysiology, then, on top of it - inferior functions gets differentiated a bit on every meditation, and, as a bonus - brains has better control over attention mechanism, which is super useful as well.

**Was this reflection necessary**

 Since sometimes I reflect on something unnecessary and waste time. But this one - is necessary. I made 80 hours per week my dream goal, while I was completely oblivious to pathological side of that goal that would've PREVENT me from doing those 80 hours. So it wasn't a goal really, but just a trap that would've injured me pretty badly. This reflection truly saved me days if not weeks of reorientation after that injury.
