### ​Discouraged

 Didn't feel that way for a long time. I expected something temptations related to come my way after I started this cold turkey thing - but this state just blows me out of the water. Everything suddenly just stopped having any meaning whatsoever. Even those aMMC timers don't help because I don't know what to do next. There's like a gorge that I need to climb down to figure the shit out. 

 Indulgence ideas also come back strong. Don't want PMO or games. But really wouldn't mind fast food or sweets, even though I'm not hungry. So today is the day when I'm forced back into old way of living. Is this consequences of cold turkey? Or because I finished major phase of the project and possibilities are overwhelming? Or both?

 Why am I discouraged and hopeless? 

\[SPOILER]

**It's only a complex**

 Ok - I must remember that it's not me. Something possesses me right now that makes me think that it's me. But it's not. Me is consistent - these states are flexible and constantly are changing. Because I identify with this state - I'm hopeless beyond hope. But if I'll detach from it - it'll help to reduce that hopelessness. And it does. 

**Why am I discouraged?**

 Indulgence comes to mind first. It's this notion that because of cold turkey I will never will be able to experience pleasure, and if I don't do cold turkey - I just exploit the shit out of it. So no shit I'm sad under these extreme choices. But here's the thing - what would it change if there was 0 indulgence in days I had meaning and was productive? Like I know I could've done it without any cost.

 But what if I had those days in the first place because of indulgence? Well, you had indulgence for like a month before "those days" - and it was pure unproductive hell. So that argument doesn't work. Indulgence isn't what made you to start working. Switching states of mind and starting to work did that. Timers. Discipline. And then you added indulgence on top of it because you thought that you can handle it since everything is so meaningful - but it bit you in the ass. 

 So, I have tasks. Before moving next I must precisely define boundaries of the app. So I would know what kind of UI I must do. I did reflect a bit on those, and this makes sense. It may be suboptimal, but it's good enough to start working instead of just staring at the wall. 

**The puer aeternus**

 Because of moments exactly like this - you're in the worst position of your life. When enthusiasm gone - you're gone with it as well. So you must train to override this behavior. This very short reflection brought back a bit of meaning, so I can ignore absence of high enthusiasm. Like all these problems are creative only on the surface. Because when I go deep - it's mechanical. Like set constraints on the app, then decide optimal layout that will be best for the app, then implement words into code. It's not a creative task, but it's a creative venture. So I don't need to be enthusiastic to solve "math problems" of isolated task. 

---

**Why I remember these states when indulgence is off the table?**

 I started to write a conclusion, but this question came to mind. Like this is very subtle feeling that happens only when I'm not having indulgence. And then when I was in bed after my nap, I almost ordered a pizza because I got this strong notion that like if I'll have it - then I'll be super productive after. Which I won't though, because from an order to an end of indulgence session - usually 1-2 hours have passed. State of mind is completely changed. You know, it's like presidents in democratic countries that try to have some agreements with a dictator. One president sets an agreement, then 4 years later another comes to power and breaks that agreement down, so how a dictator can negotiate anything if those presidents are unreliable? That "pizza promise" is the same thing.

**So what does it mean?** 

 Well, it means that the complex that makes me hopeless and instantly uses that to push me into the indulgence - should be acknowledged and handled. Seems like it's very hard to completely remove those. So my best luck is to be aware when it tries to possess me or does so already. Then it's pretty easy, surprisingly, because of this "knowing" that it's not me. Like in the sense, as if you was watching some fat dude doing those "mukbangs" or whatever, you dislike that, but you don't care because it's not me. Exactly same feeling-logic-mindset applies here as well. 

**Can I consider negative complexes as enemies?**

 Did some research, yeah - seems like it was a good thing to check. No, I can't. Because then it'll be suppressed. Ok. So what then? That complex helps me to avoid the pain at the cost of my future. Like it's no good. But is killing the future its intent? No. So it's only makes me avoid the pain, and future-thing is collateral damage. So avoiding pain only. 

 Is it a good thing still? Like I really doubt it, because I can reason through such cases to handle just enough pain to make it highly beneficial. So maybe instead of an enemy I must seek some soothing of the complex, instead of the other way around. So don't identify yourself with it, and then sooth it. Nah. Not sooth it. It's not a treatment. Talk with it. Its main goals are pain-avoidance and comfort-seeking. So you can have conversations around this topic to see where it'll lead you.

**Conclusion**

 #1 - Work. You were discouraged, but you still managed to reflect and figure out next steps. Instead of falling asleep and seeking comfort, you should've just start working. Suboptimally. Unmotivated. Without enthusiasm. 

 #2 - Your venture is creative. But each isolated task isn't. It's very procedural and "mathematical". So it means, I don't have to wait for a muse to inspire me. All I need is "cognitive honesty". So I can think and do without prejudices of the current state. **I shouldn't forget that enthusiasm is not a goal, and I must approach it as a blessing from God that comes and goes randomly.** 

 #3 - The thing that puts me in this state is my enemy. Can I consider it as an enemy though? Need to reflect. No - I can't. 

  #3.1 - First of all, stop identifying with the thing. It significantly reduces all further friction. 

  #3.2 - Talk with it. Give it a face, a name. Have a conversation around pain-avoidance and comfort-seeking.
