# Forms of Long-Term Memory

- Procedural memory
  - How to ride a bike, how to ski, how to drive
  - Embodiment of a skill
  - Stores skill independently from declarative memory's conscious recall.
    - H.M.'s case with removed hippocampus(that moves memory from present into past) allowed him still to learn some motor skills without even knowing he learned them.
- Declarative
  (remember a movie or event or some knowledge)
  - Episodic
    (involves imagery)
  - Semantic
    (language and meaning of the words)

# Procedure Before Representation

Piaget showed children can play games before they can fully explain the rules. They played well in group, but when they was separated and asked for the rules - they wasn't been able to fully explain them.
You experienced same thing with learning meaning of words in Anki, like you sometimes knew what word mean, especially in context, but wasn't able to explain that meaning.

# Children play

When kids play father and mother, they don't literally embody their parents. They have procedural knowledge of how their father acts, how "father" acts in movies and stories, they extract the pattern that unites all those fathers as spirit of father and act that out.

So that's another example how people can act out things that are deep beyond their comprehension. And they do that all the time.

Petersons uses example of stadium, where people act like one without even understanding how to act. They know what to wear, when to yell, when to cherish the athlete and so on. It isn't taught in schools, it isn't explicit, people just "know".

# Left Hemisphere Denial

Damage to the right parietal lobe often causes the left hemisphere - lacking necessary input - to assume everything is normal.
So it basically represents those emotion regulation systems, if "chaos confronting" right hemisphere won't send signals to the left that something went wrong - left hemisphere would act as if order wasn't disrupted. Like literally. On very fundamental levels. Where paralyzed person would deny that they're paralyzed.
